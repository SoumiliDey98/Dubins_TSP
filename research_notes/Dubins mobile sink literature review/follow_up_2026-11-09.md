## Kumar & Mukherjee 2026: how close is it to the draft's first objective?

### Takeaway
It is direct prior art for minimising the number of sinks. It minimises the number of mobile sinks, each limited to a total travel distance L, with tours that avoid obstacles. It has an approximation algorithm with analysis, and it serves static and mobile sensors. The abstract does not mention kinematics, dwell time or conflicts between sinks.

### Cited Findings
- [Kumar & Mukherjee 2026, COMSNETS](https://doi.org/10.1109/comsnets67989.2026.11418194) [ABS]:
  - "the total distance travelled by the MoS is considered to be no longer than a given maximum length L".
  - "we formulate an optimization problem whose objective is to minimize the number of MoSs and determine their data collection tours, which avoid any collision with obstacles in the area".
  - "An approximation algorithm has been proposed to solve the problem, and the theoretical analysis of the algorithm has been provided."
  - It is compared "with existing obstacle-aware MoS-based data collection algorithms", and is reported both to run faster and to need fewer sinks.
- It cites both Huang & Savkin 2017 and the draft's own reference, [Kumar & Mukherjee 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.adhoc.2023.103239), according to OpenAlex's citation lists.
- Authors: Rohit Kumar (SVNIT Surat) and Joy Chandra Mukherjee (IIT Bhubaneswar), from OpenAlex. Closed access, with no repository copy.
- The same group's related work, from OpenAlex [TITLE]: a vehicle-aided data collection scheme for wireless rechargeable sensor networks (COMSNETS 2021), and an approximation algorithm for vehicle path planning for data collection in such networks (ICDCN 2023).

### Inferences
- A distance limit L at constant speed equals the draft's time limit B when dwell is zero. The draft's first objective, the smallest K with every T_k <= B, is therefore the same problem apart from Dubins kinematics and dwell. The draft has to cite this paper and say what it adds: curvature, dwell, and separation between sinks.
- A reviewer from this line will ask what guarantee the draft's bisection gives, given that both Kumar & Mukherjee papers have approximation ratios. The draft currently has none.
- The commits in this repository use an IIT Bhubaneswar address, so the full text can probably be obtained from the authors directly.

### Gaps
- The obstacle model, how obstacle-avoiding distance is computed, the ratio, and whether sinks have kinematic limits or avoid one another are all unknown.

## Which newly found papers are close to the conflict game and the repair step?

### Takeaway
Picard 2022 is now the closest structural precedent for GO / WAIT / DETOUR. It uses UAVs on pre-planned 4D trajectories and discrete actions per conflict (postpone, elevate, skip), with a cost that puts conflicts first, solved by distributed algorithms that include best-response-type local search. Its vehicles can hover, so stopping is physically allowed for them, which is not true of a forward-only Dubins vehicle. MAPF-LNS2 and Potential iLQR are now confirmed in full text.

### Cited Findings
- **Discrete actions per conflict on planned trajectories.** [Picard 2022, AAMAS, pp. 1065-1073](https://ifaamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2022/pdfs/p1065.pdf) [FT]:
  - UAVs follow contracted 4D trajectories. When a conflict is detected, they choose among "postpone(c, 20), elevate(c, −15), elevate(c, +15) and skip(c)".
  - Per-action cost: "f_pref = ω² · v_conflict + ω · v_missed + v_delay", "with ω a sufficiently large number to assign preference to conflict resolution against missed way-points and delay reduction".
  - The model is a distributed constraint optimisation problem (DCOP), solved with AFB, DPOP, DSA and MGM-2 from the FRODO library, and compared with uncoordinated postpone-only and elevate-only behaviours and a centralised tree search.
  - 30 random instances per fleet size, averages with [0.05, 0.95] confidence intervals, up to dense scenarios of 20 UAVs over a 2 km × 2 km urban area.
  - It focuses on "small UAVs able to perform stationary flight".
- **Game-based conflict resolution for UAVs, en route.**
  - [Li et al. 2018, *IEEE IoT Journal* 6(2):1866-1878](https://doi.org/10.1109/jiot.2018.2885147) [ABS]: "we first formulate the CR problem as a game model and design strategies of the game model based on flight characteristics of UAVs", solved with satisficing game theory, using required time of arrival in the utility.
  - [Tony, Ghose, Chakravarthy 2020, AIAA SciTech, "CONCORD"](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2020-0876) [ABS]: conflict resolution between UAVs by correlated equilibrium.
  - Neither abstract mentions routing, pre-planned tours, or Dubins kinematics.
- **Repair loop, confirmed.** [Li et al. 2022, MAPF-LNS2, AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/21266) [FT]:
  - "Starting from a set of paths that contain collisions, MAPF-LNS2 repeatedly selects a subset of colliding agents and replans their paths to reduce the number of collisions until the paths become collision-free."
  - It "replaces the old plan P with the new plan (P \ P−) ∪ P+ iff the number of colliding pairs (CP) of the paths in the new plan is no larger than that of the old plan".
  - "Even when MAPF-LNS2 fails to find collision-free paths within the runtime limit, it usually returns paths with only a few collisions".
- **Symmetric coupling gives a potential game, confirmed.** [Kavuncu, Yaraneri, Mehr 2021, Potential iLQR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04926) [FT]:
  - "interactions where the mutual couplings between the agents are symmetric, are indeed potential differential games".
  - Equilibria come from "minimizing the potential function of the differential game", which is "a single optimal control problem".
  - It adds that a minimiser of the potential is always a Nash equilibrium, but there "may exist other equilibria".
- **Non-holonomic fleet with task assignment and collision avoidance.** [Xu et al. 2023, *IEEE T-ASE* 21(4):7027-7039](https://doi.org/10.1109/tase.2023.3336076) [ABS], [code](https://github.com/wuuya1/LRGO) [REPO]:
  - "a fleet of non-holonomic vehicles is appointed to visit a series of target positions and then move to a specific ending area".
  - Distributed task assignment (LRCA, "reducing the intersection paths between vehicles and tasks"), then distributed motion planning that "addresses the vehicle deadlocks".
  - The repository names ORCA reciprocal collision avoidance among its motion planners, and reports 50 vehicles visiting 203 targets plus real tests with 5 vehicles. The code is in Python.

### Inferences
- **The game claim must be narrower.** "A discrete wait-or-reroute choice per conflict, conflicts first" is published, in Picard 2022. And according to Chapman et al., already cited in `conflict_resolution_games.md`, DSA and MGM are potential-game best-response dynamics. What Picard does not have, and the draft does: forward-only Dubins vehicles, time-indexed tours that come from a routing step, exposure time as the shared cost, which makes the game an exact potential game, and a repair loop back into routing.
- **The repair step is MAPF-LNS2's loop.** The draft re-optimises only the sinks involved and keeps the round with the least remaining exposure. That is MAPF-LNS2's replanning of colliding agents with its "no larger number of colliding pairs" acceptance rule, with exposure in place of colliding pairs. It should be cited as such.
- **The objection "a Dubins vehicle cannot wait" is now sharper.** Every precedent found that resolves conflicts by stopping uses vehicles that can stop or hover: Picard's UAVs, Roberge & Tarbouchi in `mobile_sink_wsn.md`, and MAPF agents. The draft models forward-only Dubins vehicles but also lets them stop. This is the modelling decision already raised in `closest_prior_art.md`, objection 2.
- **Xu et al. 2023 is a ready-made baseline.** It answers the reviewer question "why not plan the tours and let reactive collision avoidance handle the rest?", and its code is public and in Python.

### Gaps
- I read Picard's model, setup and conclusions, not every result figure.
- I did not open Li et al. 2018 or CONCORD, so their strategy sets are unknown.
- I did not open Xu et al. 2023, so whether ORCA is the method used in the paper's results, and its kinematic model, are taken from the README only.

## What do the new papers say about the GA, its fitness and its seeding?

### Takeaway
No paper found seeds a GA with pyramidal tours or penalises direction reversals in a Euclidean fitness. None of the depot-related "pyramidal" hits orders cities by distance from the depot. One GA paper argues directly against a Euclidean fitness when targets are closer together than a few turning radii, which is the draft's regime.

### Cited Findings
- **Fitness on real Dubins time.** [Hansen & La Cour-Harbo 2016, ECC, pp. 2240-2246](https://doi.org/10.1109/ECC.2016.7810624); [pre-print](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/249459779/dubins_tsp.pdf) [FT]:
  - The genome holds order, heading and speed: "a genome is a sequence of genes of the form (i, φ, v)".
  - Fitness is travel time on the curves ("the time of the slowest genome of the population minus the time of the evaluated genome").
  - When waypoints are far apart compared with the turning radius, the two-step Euclidean approach "works well, but when the waypoints are closer, the kinematics of the aircraft ruins the plan".
- **Pyramidal tours in a two-vehicle problem.** [Deineko & Klinz 2018, 2VRP, arXiv 1802.08042](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08042) [FT]:
  - It uses an "optimal pyramidal tour" dynamic programme as a TSP heuristic, after Carlier & Villon.
  - Its two-tour encoding is "similar to a pyramidal tour" under a Kalmanson matrix.
  - The order is the cyclic order of points in convex position, not distance from the depot.
- **The other depot hits.**
  - [TSP on a path with precedence constraints, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1155/2016/5040513) [ABS]: the order is position along a guide wire.
  - [To walk or not to walk? 2024](https://pure.eur.nl/ws/files/163737022/1-s2.0-S1366554524002874-main.pdf) [FT, keyword scan]: "pyramidal" appears only in its reference list.
- **Generalisations of pyramidal tours.**
  - The 2024 paper cites Oda & Ota 2001, "Algorithmic aspects of pyramidal tours with restricted jump-backs" [TITLE]. Allowing a limited number of back-jumps is the closest idea found to letting a tour have m(π) > 1 peaks.
  - [The Pyramidal Method for TSP, 2000](https://doi.org/10.26552/com.c.2000.4.29-34) [ABS]: repeated shortest pyramidal tours as a heuristic for general distance matrices.
- **Searches with no relevant result** (OpenAlex, title and abstract):
  - "pyramidal tour" with genetic, evolutionary, memetic or heuristic: 2 hits, both above or unrelated.
  - TSP + GA + seeding or initial population + depot, radial, polar angle or distance from: 4 hits, all unrelated.
  - TSP or VRP fitness with a turning penalty, turn penalty, number of turns or direction changes: 0 hits.

### Inferences
- The verdict in `radially_unimodal_heuristic.md` stands. Pyramidal tours are the known class. Ranking by distance from the depot as the order, the m(π)·π·R term and seeding from such tours were not found.
- Hansen & La Cour-Harbo is the paper to cite when justifying, or questioning, a Euclidean surrogate fitness. The draft's maps are in the regime where they say the two-step approach fails, so the draft's ablation of the m(π) term (proposed in `radially_unimodal_heuristic.md`) matters more.
- Oda & Ota's "restricted jump-backs" may give a published class between pyramidal tours (one peak) and all tours. It is worth reading before the draft defines tours with a bounded number of radial peaks.

### Gaps
- I did not open Oda & Ota 2001 or the MIT 2005 thesis. Only the abstract of the AGV paper was read, because its PDF returned HTTP 403.

### Cited Findings

  - "Dubins path planning has evolved from simple analytical models to complex multirobot and dynamic scenarios. While exact solutions exist for basic tasks, the scalability, obstacle constraints and real-time responsiveness remain active challenges."
